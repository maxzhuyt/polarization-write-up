\section*{Research Question}
% ==============================================================================

Large language models trained on internet-scale corpora ingest substantial political discourse---news media, legislative records, opinion journalism, social media. We ask: \textbf{does the partisan divergence in LLM attention activations correlate with survey-measured partisan gaps across political topics?} Specifically, we compare unsupervised activation-space distances between Democrat and Republican clusters with issue-by-issue partisan gaps from the General Social Survey (GSS, 2021--2024), testing whether LLMs encode \emph{ideological} partisanship---the distance between group means on issue positions---in their internal representations.

% ==============================================================================
\section*{Method}
% ==============================================================================

For 199 GSS topics (126 public-issue, 73 private-life), we generate prompts by simulating all 550 members of the 116th U.S.\ Congress under three prompt framings (rhetorical, stance, survey). We extract per-head attention activations from the output-projection layer at the last token position, PCA-reduce each head to 15 dimensions, and compute the Mahalanobis distance between Democrat and Republican centroids. These per-head distances are averaged across all $L \times H$ heads to produce a single partisan-divergence score per topic. We then correlate this score vector with GSS survey partisanship (the normalized party-mean difference) across topics.

We test 17 transformer models (3B--72B) spanning five families, including base, instruct, reasoning, and uncensored variants. In a complementary arm, we replace politician names with demographic persona prompts constructed from real GSS respondent profiles (up to 83 attributes).

\paragraph{Scale.} Larger models achieve higher alignment: 70B+ instruct models consistently reach $r > 0.65$, while 3--9B models peak around $r = 0.65$ and are more sensitive to prompt framing.

\paragraph{Uncensored fine-tuning.} The strongest result comes from Dolphin3.0-Mistral-24B ($r = 0.74$), an uncensored fine-tune of Mistral-Small-24B with RLHF safety alignment removed. This surpasses Qwen2.5-72B ($r = 0.71$) and Llama-3.3-70B ($r = 0.65$) despite having three times fewer parameters. A controlled comparison at identical scale isolates the RLHF effect directly: Dolphin-24B-Venice, the same base model with safety alignment partially restored, achieves only $r = 0.64$ --- a gap of 0.10 points attributable purely to fine-tuning regime rather than capacity. Yi-34B-dolphin ($r = 0.72$) replicates the pattern on a different architecture. Together these results suggest that RLHF attenuates the partisan geometry encoded during pretraining rather than erasing the underlying political knowledge.

\paragraph{Private-life topics.} Alignment extends to private-life topics (religious practice, sexual attitudes, moral views). Llama-3.1-8B instruct achieves $r = 0.69$ on private topics---exceeding its own public-issue best---suggesting the model has internalized the broader cultural sorting of American society, not just policy positions.

\paragraph{Demographic simulation.} Replacing politician names with demographic persona prompts yields weaker alignment ($r \leq 0.51$, even with 83-attribute profiles from real GSS respondents), indicating that models encode partisan structure more reliably through named-entity associations than through demographic inference.

% ==============================================================================
\section*{Discussion}
% ==============================================================================

The finding that a 24B uncensored model outperforms safety-aligned models at three times the scale --- and that restoring partial alignment to the same base model at identical scale costs 0.10 correlation points --- is scientifically informative but ethically complex. We do not recommend uncensored models for deployment; we report this because it reveals that RLHF safety training measurably affects the discriminative geometry of partisan representations. Higher alignment does not imply a ``better'' model---it indicates that the activation structure more closely mirrors survey data.

More broadly, these results document that LLMs trained on political discourse develop internal representations whose partisan structure tracks real-world opinion data. This complements supervised approaches to partisan representation learning \citep{kim2025linear} by showing that unsupervised activation geometry---computed without any training signal---correlates with external survey measures.

% ==============================================================================
\section*{Limitations}
% ==============================================================================

The study is US-centric; generalization to multiparty systems is untested. Correlations are ecological (topic-level, $N = 126$ or 73), not individual-level. We measure ideological partisanship only (group-mean distance), not affective partisan sentiment or distributional bimodality. Point estimates lack uncertainty quantification; bootstrap confidence intervals are planned.
